# Epistemic Trust Lab

**Spencer H. Walker** · Independent research software · November 2026  
**Research areas:** Human–AI interaction, information science, epistemology, trustworthy AI, cognitive science, and computational evaluation.

> **When does an AI system deserve a user's confidence, and how can we separate its apparent understanding from its measured reliability?**

## Research objective

Epistemic Trust Lab is a locally running research instrument for examining conversational context and evidence quality. The system is designed to help distinguish **observable system behavior** from subjective perceptions of intelligence, competence, and trustworthiness. It provides controlled, inspectable test fixtures rather than treating conversational fluency as evidence of genuine understanding.

## What the current evaluations do and do not establish

**Implemented and locally evaluated:**
- Deterministic evaluation policies applied to synthetic, privacy-safe scenarios.
- A testable workflow for inspecting context and supporting evidence.
- Quantitative summaries, uncertainty intervals, and exported evaluation data.
- A researcher-facing interface and automated test coverage.

**Not established:**
- Performance or generalization of live language models: **real-model validation is future work**.
- Effects on actual users' trust, intelligence judgments, or reliance: **no authorized human-participant study has been completed**.
- Clinical, psychological, or causal conclusions from synthetic fixtures.
- External peer review or independent replication of a public source release.

The current 5,760 evaluations are synthetic-policy runs, **not 5,760 human trials or model conversations**.

## Planned research progression

1. Release the documented source code, reproducible benchmark fixtures, tests, and technical report after reviewing the archive for secrets, personal data, third-party materials, and license restrictions.
2. Extend the harness to a predeclared comparison of real models and memory conditions with a held-out evaluation set.
3. Distinguish response quality from response presentation when testing conversational realism.
4. Develop a consent-based, institutionally authorized human-participant protocol before making claims about trust calibration, perceived understanding, or user behavior.
5. Publish exact configuration, raw or appropriately de-identified outputs, uncertainty estimates, limitations, and negative findings for inspection.
